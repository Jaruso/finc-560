# Week 6 — Interactive Treasury Yield Dashboard Design

**Dashboard:** https://jaruso.github.io/finc-560/week-06/  
**Audience:** Fixed-income analysts evaluating interest-rate exposure under hypothetical monetary-policy scenarios.  
**Data:** Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED: [DGS5](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS5), [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), [DFF](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF).

The Treasury Yield Curve Scenario Lab gives fixed-income analysts one workspace in which to evaluate how changing monetary-policy assumptions could affect the five-year yield, ten-year yield, and the spread between them. It is an interactive sensitivity model, not a forecasting or investment recommendation system. Its visual priority is to let an analyst see how a hypothetical future develops directly from actual recent market observations, without scrolling or visually disconnected timelines.

Three selectable visualizations use **one continuous calendar x-axis** per graph. Each graph contains observed history, a central illustrative future path, and upper and lower sensitivity paths. The five-year and ten-year charts focus on the maturity-specific response; the 10Y–5Y spread chart adds a zero-inversion reference line. A shaded background begins at the exact last observed monthly data point, distinguishing the hypothetical period. All traces use the same calendar units and the same y-axis, so changing the history window or projection horizon naturally repositions the historical-to-hypothetical boundary. The audience is never shown a ten-year historical period and a six-month hypothetical period occupying equal horizontal space.

This choice creates a deliberate readability trade-off: a six-month scenario occupies little space beside ten years of actual observations. Rather than distort time, the **Focus projection** control zooms to the most recent observed months and the forward scenario while retaining the identical calendar scale. The history selector supports one, two, five, ten, fifteen, twenty, or all available years; the scenario horizon supports six, twelve, or twenty-four months. Zooming changes the visible window, not the underlying history, projections, or data provenance.

Interaction centers on choosing a single relevant metric and directly changing modeling assumptions. A narrow controls rail contains the hypothetical cumulative policy-rate adjustment, separate responses for the two maturity yields, the projection horizon, and an illustrative uncertainty-width slider. A compact KPI row summarizes observed and hypothetical values, while the large graph updates after each interaction. The separate historical-fit disclosure can populate response sliders using observational monthly co-movements; these estimates must not be mistaken for causal reactions to unexpected policy announcements.

The projected lines represent a user-specified linear policy path multiplied by the separate response assumptions. Their bounds are manual **what-if sensitivities**, not empirical confidence intervals, standard deviations, or predicted probabilities. Observed history stays unchanged when controls move, and the real FRED snapshot records its latest synchronized observation and retrieval date. This design helps an analyst investigate plausible *conditional* yield-curve implications without presenting arbitrary scenario assumptions as facts.
