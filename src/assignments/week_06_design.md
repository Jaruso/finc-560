# Week 6 — Treasury Yield Curve Forecasting Dashboard

**Intended audience:** Fixed-income analysts evaluating interest-rate exposure and yield-curve shape.  
**Dashboard:** https://jaruso.github.io/finc-560/week-06/  
**Data sources:** Federal Reserve / FRED [DGS1](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS1), [DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2), [DGS3](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS3), [DGS5](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS5), [DGS7](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS7), [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), [DGS20](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS20), [DGS30](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS30), and [DFF](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF).

This dashboard has evolved from a manual yield-curve simulator into a data-calibrated forecasting and conditional-analysis tool. Its decision problem is whether alternative interest-rate scenarios materially change the projected relative movement of five- and ten-year Treasury yields and the resulting inversion risk. Observations, fitted baseline forecasts, and analyst-imposed scenarios are explicitly distinguished. The models are research tools, not predictions of actual Federal Reserve decisions or investment recommendations.

The **forecasting engine** uses a fixed-decay Nelson–Siegel representation of the entire observed Treasury curve. Each month, the eight available constant maturities are taken from the same common trading day and used to estimate level, slope, and curvature factors by cross-sectional least squares. Separate monthly AR(1) processes forecast these three factors up to 24 months, creating a simplified two-stage *Diebold–Li-style* baseline. The latest partial month, when available, can anchor the current cross-sectional estimate but is excluded from full-month dynamics estimation and historical validation. The output is automatically regenerated from real FRED observations by a weekday GitHub Actions job; it is not a continuous intraday feed.

The dashboard offers two selectable visualizations on an **undistorted continuous calendar axis**. The first overlays observed five- and ten-year yields and continues each with a dashed fitted-model path. The second shows their difference and a clearly identified zero-inversion threshold. Changing the historical display window or projection horizon naturally moves the boundary between observations and modeled data. Focus Projection zooms into recent months when a six-month forecast would otherwise appear extremely narrow beside a decade of history.

Interactivity is grounded in model outputs rather than arbitrary yield multipliers. The forecast-horizon selector displays six-, twelve-, or twenty-four-month estimates. An optional policy slider specifies a **one-time next-month effective-funds-rate deviation relative to the statistical baseline**. It changes the forecast through empirically fitted associations between monthly policy-rate changes and changes in yield-curve factors, propagated by the fitted AR dynamics. That association is observational, not a causal estimate of surprise Fed action; rates already anticipated by markets may not affect yields similarly.

The shaded ranges come from historical 10th–90th percentiles of **expanding rolling-origin out-of-sample forecast errors**. In addition to the separate maturity errors, spread error is calibrated directly so the bounds account for co-movement. These descriptive intervals are not guaranteed future probabilities, especially after the optional policy adjustment. The collapsed validation panel reports the model's historical 6-, 12-, and 24-month RMSE beside a simple unchanged-yield benchmark. Underperformance is displayed rather than hidden, allowing analysts to judge whether the additional model complexity has demonstrated value.


## October 2026: current multi-asset dashboard architecture

The original stakeholder is a **fixed-income analyst** evaluating the yield curve and interest-rate scenarios. The Bonds workspace supplies the primary evidence through eight selectable views. Equities and Commodities offer parallel, source-specific secondary analysis; each workspace supports a coordinated layout of one, two, three or four related visualizations on the same canvas. Four charts are selected by default to demonstrate the assignment's minimum without relying on separate tabs or thousands of ticker configurations.

### Equities: verified financial data and related visualizations

The public GitHub Pages frontend requests current quotes, company profiles and as-reported financial statements through a tightly allowlisted Cloudflare Worker. Finnhub's API key remains a server-side secret. Dates, report types, financial tags and annual coverage are checked by a deterministic normalization layer. Where Finnhub's filing history is older or incomplete, the newest published, verified financial snapshot is retained; dates and reporting periods are labeled. The forecast canvas offers eight user-selectable views: the focused forecast, revenue, operating earnings, profit margins, cash flow versus capital expenditure, free cash flow, interest coverage and disclosed cash versus debt. Options requiring unavailable input fields are disabled rather than fabricating figures. CAGR, OLS and scenario adjustments are explicitly differentiated from reported observations.

The lower section now displays **recent company news**, not duplicate financial research cards. A separate Finnhub /company-news request returns up to 15 articles for the selected ticker covering the preceding 30 days, refreshed manually or after 15 minutes when visible. Headlines identify the publisher, timestamp and original HTTPS article, with optional summaries. News is contextual third-party coverage, not automatically ranked as financially significant and **not counted** toward the minimum of four visualizations. The news feed does not affect the forecast calculation, and an unavailable news subscription does not block existing annual charts.

### Decision-usefulness and limitations

For the fixed-income audience, the Bonds views reveal current term structure, inversion, conditional interest-rate sensitivity and rolling out-of-sample forecast error. Equity profitability, free-cash-flow and disclosed-coverage trends can help contextualize issuer financial resilience, but available financial statements do not establish actual refinancing terms, interest-rate hedges or credit spreads. The Commodities tab illustrates spot-price and scenario behavior, with historic returns and realized volatility. Financial statements are dated, Finnhub coverage and plan permissions vary by issuer, and market news should be corroborated at the publisher rather than treated as model input or verified disclosures. This ensures the dashboards' related visualizations communicate distinct financial insights for a stakeholder while preserving strict provenance between observed facts, model assumptions and media reports.
