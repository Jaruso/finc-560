# Week 6 — Interactive Financial Dashboard Design Document

**Dashboard:** https://jaruso.github.io/finc-560/week-06/  
**Audience:** Fixed-income investment analysts evaluating interest-rate exposure.  
**Sources:** Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED [DGS5](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS5), [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), and [DFF](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF).  
**Interpretation:** An interactive *scenario* dashboard, not an investment forecast.

The Treasury Yield Curve Scenario Lab is designed for an analyst monitoring how monetary policy and different market responses might affect the relative pricing of intermediate- and long-duration Treasury securities. This analyst needs to identify periods of historical curve inversion, compare maturities, and test specific assumptions before evaluating portfolio duration exposure. In particular, the decision is not simply whether policy rates will rise or fall: it is how differently the five- and ten-year securities might respond and what those differences would imply for the curve.

Four visualizations support this workflow on a single dashboard canvas. The first displays historical 5-year and 10-year Treasury yields, establishing the direction and magnitude of changes in each maturity. The second isolates the 10Y-minus-5Y spread, with a zero reference line and red markers for observed inversions. Together, they help the analyst distinguish broad yield movements from changes in curve shape. The third chart shows separate hypothetical yield paths under user-controlled policy and sensitivity assumptions. The fourth translates those paths into a hypothetical spread, including the zero-inversion threshold and a clearly labeled, user-defined sensitivity corridor. These last two visualizations support scenario comparison without presenting an assumed response as an actual forecast.

The information hierarchy puts baseline yields, the observed spread, and the terminal scenario spread immediately above the four charts. This allows the analyst to recognize the initial market condition and the scenario outcome before reviewing the supporting detail. A restrained navy-and-teal palette consistently distinguishes the maturities; red marks the inversion threshold and actual historical inversion observations. Line charts are appropriate for the chronological series and assumed trajectories, while the shaded corridor shows the range implied by a *manual sensitivity setting*. It is deliberately not labeled a confidence interval or an inversion probability.

Interactivity changes the analytical question rather than merely changing presentation. The historical-window control filters both observational charts and recalculates the displayed historical co-movement diagnostic. The cumulative policy-rate adjustment slider, projection horizon, and two independent maturity-response sliders drive both hypothetical charts simultaneously. An additional corridor slider tests how fragile the assumed terminal spread is to alternative shocks. A separate button can copy the fitted historical response estimates into the manual assumptions, but the dashboard explains that contemporaneous observational relationships are not causal policy effects. Plotly tooltips provide the original numeric values without overloading the chart labels.

All historical observations come from FRED using the existing Python provider and a versioned JSON export refreshed by GitHub Actions. The snapshot includes retrieval and latest-observation dates. The scenario applies a deliberately simple linear cumulative policy adjustment over the selected horizon; each yield changes according to its independently adjustable response coefficient. Actual market yields also reflect expected policy, term premia, macroeconomic news, and many other influences, so a modeled zero crossing is an illustration under assumptions—not a prediction of timing, recession, or investment performance.
