# Week 6 — Treasury Yield Curve Forecasting Dashboard

**Intended audience:** Fixed-income analysts evaluating interest-rate exposure and yield-curve shape.  
**Dashboard:** https://jaruso.github.io/finc-560/week-06/  
**Data sources:** Federal Reserve / FRED [DGS1](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS1), [DGS2](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS2), [DGS3](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS3), [DGS5](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS5), [DGS7](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS7), [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), [DGS20](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS20), [DGS30](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS30), and [DFF](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF).

This dashboard has evolved from a manual yield-curve simulator into a data-calibrated forecasting and conditional-analysis tool. Its decision problem is whether alternative interest-rate scenarios materially change the projected relative movement of five- and ten-year Treasury yields and the resulting inversion risk. Observations, fitted baseline forecasts, and analyst-imposed scenarios are explicitly distinguished. The models are research tools, not predictions of actual Federal Reserve decisions or investment recommendations.

The **forecasting engine** uses a fixed-decay Nelson–Siegel representation of the entire observed Treasury curve. Each month, the eight available constant maturities are taken from the same common trading day and used to estimate level, slope, and curvature factors by cross-sectional least squares. Separate monthly AR(1) processes forecast these three factors up to 24 months, creating a simplified two-stage *Diebold–Li-style* baseline. The latest partial month, when available, can anchor the current cross-sectional estimate but is excluded from full-month dynamics estimation and historical validation. The output is automatically regenerated from real FRED observations by a weekday GitHub Actions job; it is not a continuous intraday feed.

The dashboard offers two selectable visualizations on an **undistorted continuous calendar axis**. The first overlays observed five- and ten-year yields and continues each with a dashed fitted-model path. The second shows their difference and a clearly identified zero-inversion threshold. Changing the historical display window or projection horizon naturally moves the boundary between observations and modeled data. Focus Projection zooms into recent months when a six-month forecast would otherwise appear extremely narrow beside a decade of history.

Interactivity is grounded in model outputs rather than arbitrary yield multipliers. The forecast-horizon selector displays six-, twelve-, or twenty-four-month estimates. An optional policy slider specifies a **one-time next-month effective-funds-rate deviation relative to the statistical baseline**. It changes the forecast through empirically fitted associations between monthly policy-rate changes and changes in yield-curve factors, propagated by the fitted AR dynamics. That association is observational, not a causal estimate of surprise Fed action; rates already anticipated by markets may not affect yields similarly.

The shaded ranges come from historical 10th–90th percentiles of **expanding rolling-origin out-of-sample forecast errors**. In addition to the separate maturity errors, spread error is calibrated directly so the bounds account for co-movement. These descriptive intervals are not guaranteed future probabilities, especially after the optional policy adjustment. The collapsed validation panel reports the model's historical 6-, 12-, and 24-month RMSE beside a simple unchanged-yield benchmark. Underperformance is displayed rather than hidden, allowing analysts to judge whether the additional model complexity has demonstrated value.


## Extension: corporate credit research in the Equities workspace (October 2026)

The original **fixed-income analyst** audience and yield-curve inversion decision problem remain unchanged. The Equities extension asks a related issuer-level question: **How would a range of modeled Treasury-rate environments affect a company's illustrative financing burden and debt-service capacity?** This is scenario exploration, not a finding that changes in Treasury yields *cause* changes in any issuer's borrowing rate.

### Data acquisition and provenance
A GitHub Pages frontend requests four tightly allowlisted endpoints from a Cloudflare Worker holding the Finnhub token in a server-side secret: quote (short-lived refresh), company profile, basic financial metrics, and annual as-reported filings. On ticker selection the frontend retrieves the last three in parallel; the quote refreshes independently. The annual SEC normalization layer accepts *only* supported USD US-GAAP concepts in annual 10-K/10-K/A filings, links every row to its SEC accession and rejects duplicate fiscal years. It does not fill in missing totals, financial expenses, cash flows or debt maturities. If fewer than five comparable reported years are verified, the existing CAGR and OLS forecasts stay disabled for a new ticker; the original verified featured snapshots are retained when an API response is incomplete, unavailable or older. A market quote and a full fundamental dataset have different freshness characteristics and are never labeled interchangeably.

### Visualization, insight and decision needs

1. **Financial resilience**: reported annual free cash flow and interest expense (in the same USD-billions unit) provide historical context, alongside operating margin, interest coverage, and net-debt-to-EBITDA *only when each input is present*. This helps a fixed-income analyst identify a shift in the issuer's ability to service debt. Negative free cash flow remains visible.
2. **Treasury-linked stress**: the published FRED-trained Macro workspace's *dated* latest and modeled 12-month 10-year Treasury baseline feeds a conditional corporate interest-coverage sensitivity chart. The analyst explicitly chooses a credit-spread assumption, a 10-year yield shock and the assumed fraction of outstanding debt that reprices over the scenario window. The resulting change in annual interest expense is applied to the last reported interest cost. Observed income and debt are held fixed. Debt maturity, floating-rate exposure, refinancing timing, and the relationship between the Treasury and issuer yield curves are **not inferred** from the API. If reported total debt or interest expense is missing, the result is unavailable.
3. **Supplemental equity sensitivity**: an explicitly simplified five-year enterprise cash-flow scenario projects a transparent free-cash-flow-to-the-firm *proxy* (operating cash minus capital expenditure plus assumed after-tax interest). It discounts five forecast flows and terminal value at a *user-assumed* discount rate; the resulting enterprise value is adjusted by latest disclosed net debt and divided by reported shares outstanding. The discount-rate versus FCFF-growth heatmap demonstrates sensitivity, not a price target or a complete investment valuation. No numerical valuation is calculated if any required source is absent or starting FCFF proxy is non-positive.

**Limitations**: As-reported tags differ by filer; industry-specific lenders, IFRS reporters and issuers without the required tags may show unavailable analyses. Finnhub's API plan determines financial statement availability. Historical financial ratios may not be comparable across fiscal calendars. The published Treasury model is observational and out-of-sample uncertainty is not automatically transferred to company debt costs. Assumed 21% tax, default 40% exposed debt and 200-basis-point spread are modeling settings, not disclosed issuer facts. The valuation excludes detailed debt schedules, share dilution, tax shields beyond the stated simple assumption and a full working-capital forecast. The two original dashboards' forecast and validation methods are preserved.
