# Week 6 — Interactive Treasury Yield Dashboard Design

**Dashboard:** https://jaruso.github.io/finc-560/week-06/  
**Audience:** Fixed-income analysts evaluating hypothetical maturity-specific yield responses.  
**Sources:** Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis FRED [DGS5](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS5), [DGS10](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), and [DFF](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DFF).

The Treasury Yield Curve Scenario Lab helps analysts investigate how different rate-adjustment assumptions could affect five- and ten-year Treasury yields and the spread between them. It is a sensitivity tool, not a forecasting system. Its central design decision is to place actual historical observations immediately beside hypothetical outcomes. Analysts can see the starting conditions and the consequences of changing assumptions without scrolling between separate charts.

Three selectable *compound* charts each contain two distinct analytical views. Five-year and ten-year yield charts show their own historical trajectories on the left and central, upper, and lower hypothetical yield paths on the right. The spread chart similarly places the observed 10Y–5Y spread beside its hypothetical path, shading an illustrative sensitivity range and marking the zero-inversion threshold. These six related views establish historical context and support comparisons between assumptions. The two chart halves occupy equal visual width and share a vertical scale, but **their date axes are independent**: two years of observations, for example, must not appear to cover the same elapsed time as a one-year scenario. Each half is explicitly labeled.

Interaction centers on two decisions. A single-chart switcher offers one large, legible plot; Compare Two enables analysts to study any pair simultaneously. A separate historical context selector changes only the amount of observed data displayed. Sliders modify the cumulative assumed policy change, separate maturity response factors, scenario horizon, and a user-defined sensitivity envelope. Visible projected paths redraw immediately without changing FRED observations. Historical regression estimates are available behind an optional disclosure and can populate the manual response settings, but remain labeled as observational co-movement rather than causal estimates.

The projected central line is the result of a linear, assumed rate adjustment multiplied by each maturity's selected response factor. The envelope is **not** a statistical standard deviation, confidence interval, probability, or observed market range. Upper and lower five-year and ten-year yield paths vary by half of the selected basis-point sensitivity width; the spread varies by the full width, consistent with opposite-direction maturity shocks. The filled band minimizes clutter while showing how different assumptions influence the modeled curve.

Compact controls, small summary indicators, consistent color assignments, and a single dominant chart prioritize interpretation over decoration. Clearly distinguishing observed data from hypothetical paths is essential: an analyst should identify potential inversion *under stated assumptions*, not mistake an illustrative zero crossing for an investment recommendation, predicted rate path, or recession forecast. The underlying FRED snapshot and source date are displayed separately from the interactive calculations.
