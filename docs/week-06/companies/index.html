Revenue forecasting methods

Historical CAGR (compound annual growth rate) calculates the constant annual percentage change that connects the first and last revenue observations in the most recent five reported fiscal years. It compounds that percentage from the latest actual revenue for each projected year. This assumes a consistent proportional growth rate; it may exaggerate future growth when a company is maturing and is sensitive to the selected period's starting and ending years.

Linear trend (OLS, ordinary least squares) fits a straight line through up to six recent annual revenue observations, minimizing the sum of squared differences between the line and reported values. It extends the fitted trend as an approximately fixed dollar change each year rather than a fixed percentage. Projected revenue is floored at zero. The fit may be influenced by unusually strong or weak years and may miss compounding growth patterns.

Scenario adjustments: The annual growth slider compounds an additional percentage-point adjustment onto the selected model's revenue path. The profit-margin slider adds percentage points to the three-year, recency-weighted operating and net profit margins used to estimate projected earnings; it does not change revenue. The solid line shows reported financials, the dashed line shows the adjusted forecast, and a dotted baseline appears when assumptions change.

Both methods extrapolate historical financials under explicit assumptions. Neither is an analyst consensus estimate, a prediction interval, or a guarantee of future performance.

Data and methods

Initial featured-company figures come from directly linked public annual reports and SEC 10-K disclosures, with the latest source linked for each company. The Microsoft FY2026 source is its official annual earnings release. These are verified snapshots; automated SEC refresh is paused while hosted-runner access returns 403. No synthetic company data are published.

Revenue forecasts use an OLS trend or a CAGR fitted to recent annual revenue. Operating and net margins use three-year recency-weighted historical averages. Growth adjustments compound relative to the selected baseline, while the margin adjustment changes both derived projected profit margins by the stated percentage points. This is a driver-based conditional projection, not a probability distribution or a complete integrated financial-statement model.

Fiscal-calendar differences and amendments affect comparability. The annual financial figures above remain dated verified issuer/SEC snapshots. A separate Finnhub market quote is requested when you choose a company and approximately once per minute while this tab is visible. Market quotes may be delayed, particularly outside trading hours, and do not feed into or alter the annual revenue and profit forecasts. The dashboard calls a restricted Cloudflare Worker; the Finnhub API key stays server-side and is never published in the browser or this repository.
